# Median holds up, but the tail and silence hurt

Our median latency is competitive; our tail and our silent wait are the genuine problems. The p50 of **10.4s** sits right on Nielsen's 10-second attention limit, inside the 9s condition that a 2026 CHI study rated best, in the same range as Intercom Fin's reported ~7s median, and far faster than finance research agents (mean 91-109s). The p90 of **26.8s** and p95 of **32.3s** are 2.7x and 3.2x that limit, fall outside the 2-20s range any controlled study tested, exceed Amazon Lex's 30s default fulfilment window, and are 3-3.5x slower than search-grounded assistants such as Perplexity Sonar at p50 and p95. The decisive factor is not the raw number but the delivery: the response is one non-streamed JSON, so users see nothing for the whole wait, and Nielsen's guidance for waits over 10s is explicit progress feedback. The October sample (n=26) is worse (p50 14.2s, p90 56.9s, p95 58.3s, within about 2s of the ALB's 60s idle timeout), but it is too small to call a trend, and the hypothesis that Google's silent re-routing of gemini-3.7-flash to 3.8-flash caused it is unconfirmed for our traffic. No major vendor publishes a chatbot latency SLA, so "industry benchmark" here means a patchwork of HCI thresholds, one controlled study, third-party measurements and anecdotes. The cheapest high-value fix is to send status events (first feedback within about a second, plus ALB keep-alive), followed by tail diagnosis and a model-routing check.

## The median is fine because most of it is unavoidable model time

The strongest argument that p50 is not a problem comes from the arithmetic of what the models must do. Our synthesis call generates roughly 500-800 answer tokens plus 600-1,000 thinking tokens, and at the 140-340 tokens per second Artificial Analysis reports for Gemini Flash models, that is 3.2-12.9 seconds of pure generation per call before any time to first token ([AA 3.5 Flash](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-3-5-flash/providers), [AA 3.8 Flash](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-3-8-flash/providers)). Adding a short router call, the researchers' typical scenario totals about **10.5s**, essentially our observed 10.4s; the best case is about 5.4s and the pessimistic case about 18.9s. This is an inference with a soft input (the 200-token router output is assumed), but it implies that the median has little infrastructure slack to recover. Whatever improves p50 must reduce tokens generated, remove a serial LLM call, or use a faster model.

The human-perception evidence also favours the median. The CHI 2026 study (NYU Tandon, n=240 per a press summary) fixed time-to-first-token at 2, 9 or 20 seconds and found that participants rated the 2s replies *less* thoughtful and useful than the 9s and 20s ones, and read delays mostly as deliberation ([arXiv 2604.06183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06183)). Intercom ran a first-party experiment adding 5-20s of artificial delay to Fin; resolution rates rose by up to 2 percentage points, negative feedback did not increase and CSAT was unchanged ([Intercom Research](https://fin.ai/research/does-slower-seem-smarter-rethinking-latency-in-ai-agents/)). The honest reading is that a ~10s wait is not penalised by users in either study. Two limits apply: both are measured on streamed or conversational support settings rather than a non-streamed financial lookup, and the CHI study's "peak at 9s" detail comes from a press summary rather than the paper text the researchers retrieved.

Against products users compare us to, the median is unremarkable. Intercom reports a ~7s median start of response after a year of optimisation, though that is first token, whereas ours is the finished answer ([Intercom Research](https://fin.ai/research/think-fast-reasoning-at-3ms-a-token)). The only direct anecdotes for NL-to-data tools put Databricks Genie near 20s for a trivial prompt and Looker Conversational Analytics near 40s for a simple question, with one user stating the expected experience is under 10s ([Databricks Community](https://community.databricks.com/t5/generative-ai/how-does-genie-look-under-the-hood/m-p/157395), [Google Developer Forum](https://discuss.google.dev/t/looker-conversational-analytics-high-latency/392507)). These are single-user reports and should not be treated as product benchmarks. Finance incumbents (Bloomberg ASKB, FactSet Mercury, AlphaSense, Capital IQ, Morningstar, Perplexity Finance, Copilot for Finance) publish no latency figures at all, so no direct finance-assistant comparison exists.

## The tail and the silent wait are the genuine problems

Nielsen's classic guidance sets 10 seconds as the limit for holding attention and prescribes a percent-done indicator or, for agent work of unknown length, a live step list with elapsed time rather than a bare spinner ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/), [UX Tigers](https://www.uxtigers.com/post/progress-indicators)). Our p90 and p95 are 2.7x and 3.2x that limit, and no controlled study covers waits past 20s: at 20s the CHI participants already sometimes shifted to frustration or doubts about reliability. Evidence that tolerance extends to 30s+ for a non-streamed answer is absent, so the tail is where user concern is most plausibly justified. The researchers could not determine what the front end currently shows during the wait (not in scope of the notes); if it shows only a spinner, users experience the full 10-58s with no information, which is the exact regime Nielsen warns against.

Against peers with a search-chat product shape the gap is real: Perplexity's Sonar API shows a p50 of 3.16s and p95 of 9.09s on Eden AI's real traffic ([Eden AI](https://www.edenai.co/models/sonar)), so we are roughly 3.3x slower at the median and 3.6x at p95. Our work (BigQuery plus two thinking-enabled LLM calls) is more than a search call, so the comparison indicates user expectations rather than a target we can match. A related ceiling comparison: Amazon Lex's default 30-second fulfilment window would already time out at our p95 ([AWS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/lexv2/latest/dg/intent-fulfillment)), though that is a platform default and not a service-level target.

The sharpest evidence of a tail problem is our own. In the load test (single uvicorn worker), the service saturated at concurrency 32 with p95 around 60s, where the ALB's 60-second idle timeout converts slowness into 504 errors. The CloudWatch alarm documentation records "normal chat/synthesis calls run 7-40s", with one 180s outlier on Aug 11. By month, August showed p50 14.1s and p95 33.2s, September p50 8.7s and p95 25.1s, and October p50 14.2s, p90 56.9s and p95 58.3s. The October sample is only **n=26**, so its p95 rests on one or two requests and the apparent regression could be noise. One hypothesis for a real change: Google's docs, last updated 2026-10-13, say requests to `gemini-3.7-flash` are automatically routed to `gemini-3.8-flash`, which Artificial Analysis currently measures slower (137-144 tokens per second and a ~25s time to first answer token at its high setting, versus a 340 tokens per second snapshot for 3.7) and describes as very verbose ([Google docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/whats-new-gemini-3.7), [AA 3.8 Flash](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-3-8-flash/providers), [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-3-8-flash-review)). This is **unconfirmed for our traffic**: the researchers did not find the date routing began, and AA's high-thinking figures reflect 2,400-3,400 thinking tokens, far more than our LOW setting, so they must not be read as our model's behaviour.

Provider variance is a second candidate. No tracker publishes p95 (Artificial Analysis reports P50 only, and only for Google AI Studio). The one available tail evidence is a single forum user on 2026-09-01 measuring one-word replies to gemini-3.7-flash at 2-27s on the Gemini API versus 1.4-3.8s on Vertex global, with three calls per cell ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/high-latency-for-ai-studio-gemini-3-7-flash/180447)). That is an anecdote, not a benchmark, but because our pipeline makes two sequential model calls it would compound two draws from any such tail. This fits a 32-58s p95 better than steady-state throughput does, and it remains a hypothesis.

## What is measured, what is claimed, and what is only anecdote

Keeping evidence classes separate matters here because the most-quoted figures are the weakest. **Measured:** our own percentiles (internal data); Nielsen's thresholds (decades of HCI, though 1993-era and not LLM-specific); the CHI 2026 study (peer-reviewed venue, but time-to-first-token with likely streaming); Intercom's A/B test (first-party, support chat); Perplexity Sonar percentiles (third-party gateway traffic); text-to-SQL and finance-agent papers (academic, different architectures); Artificial Analysis medians (P50 over 72 hours, 10k-token prompt). **Vendor claims:** the 1-2s enterprise RAG target, Intercom's ~7s median (first-party but self-reported), Fintool's ~40s average, deep-research durations, and the OpenAI rule that cutting 50% of output tokens cuts roughly 50% of latency while halving input tokens yields 1-5% ([OpenAI latency guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latency-optimization)). **Anecdotes:** Genie, Looker, the Gemini forum threads and Intercom community guidance of ~7s per reasoning pass.

Several caveats must travel with these figures. Many documents were read through a summarising fetch tool or via search-result summaries rather than in full, including the Neuroscience News piece on the CHI study, the PMC and IEEE papers, SmartScope, Flightcontrol, and the Fintool and Eden AI pages, so exact figures should be verified before external use. Artificial Analysis publishes P50 only, and the fetched pages sometimes disagree with secondary sources (for example, 3.8 Flash at 137-144 tokens per second today versus 305 in a September review). **No published chatbot latency SLAs were found** from Gartner, Forrester, Intercom, Zendesk, Drift or Salesforce; this reflects the search budget, not confirmed absence. Nielsen's progress-indicators page is dated "Aug 8" with the year inferred as 2026 by the fetch tool, so treat the date as unverified. The 2023 AI response times (ChatGPT 4.0 41.3s, Bing Chat 13.0s, Perplexity 7.4s, Google SGE 4.4s) predate reasoning models and are included only as history. Several figures were deliberately excluded as untraceable or unverified: a "2025 Journal of Consumer Research" per-5-seconds message-drop statistic that the researchers could not trace to the original paper, a "68% of production RAG deployments exceed 2s P95" claim, a Genie "<2 second" aggregator summary, and the uncited "streaming feels 40-60% faster" assertion. Google's mobile page-load abandonment figures (53% at 3s) concern web pages, not chatbots, and are not used as a benchmark. The "3x longer willingness to wait" progress-bar result is one pre-LLM lab study relayed secondhand through NN/g ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/)).

## Prioritised recommendations, with evidence strength

All recommendations are grounded in the notes; none were tested on our system, and magnitudes of savings for our pipeline are unmeasured unless stated. Evidence strength reflects how well the underlying claim is supported, not how large the benefit will be.

| # | Recommendation | Why (from the notes) | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Emit status or stream events** at stage boundaries ("routing", "querying financial data", "writing answer"), with a first event within about 1s of submit; also use them to keep the ALB connection alive | Nielsen: past 10s show a live step list and elapsed time, not a bare spinner; honest stage labels, no fake animations ([UX Tigers](https://www.uxtigers.com/post/progress-indicators)). AWS: send at least 1 byte before each idle timeout elapses ([AWS ALB docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/elasticloadbalancing/latest/application/edit-load-balancer-attributes.html)). Perception benefit: moderate (generic HCI; no controlled LLM study; pre-LLM 3x wait result is secondhand). Caveat: check for Service Connect or other intermediaries, as one single-author report found a hidden 15s idle timer ([Oliverio](https://www.oliverio.dev/blog/aws-service-connect-sse)). |
| 2 | **Investigate the tail**: add per-phase timings (router, query, synthesis), segment slow requests by phase, and re-measure October with a larger sample | October p90 56.9s and p95 58.3s on n=26 are untrustworthy; alarm doc mentions a 180s outlier; p50 is explained by model time but the tail is not. Faster rows on AA: 3.5 Flash minimal (0.84s TTFT, 3.44s end-to-end for 500 tokens), 3.5 Flash-Lite (433 tok/s), and non-Gemini low-effort options (Sonnet 5.5 low 5.99s, GPT-6 Luna low 6.58s). Caveats: quality for financial accuracy is untested, models deprecate every 1-2 months, MINIMAL support on 3.7/3.8 is unconfirmed, and AA rows are P50 only. Priority tier looks like a poor bet: qualitative claims, 3.7 not on the supported list, one test showed no benefit. | Routing claim: moderate (Google docs) but unconfirmed for our traffic. Speed ranking: moderate (AA P50). Priority-tier skepticism: weak (single forum test). |
| 4 | **Reduce thinking and output length** on the synthesis call: test a lower thinking level where supported, and ask for sentence- or paragraph-limited answers | Latency scales roughly linearly with output tokens (OpenAI); thinking is about 40-55% of our generated tokens (arithmetic on our token counts); at ~200 tok/s, 600-1,000 thinking tokens is 3-5s per call. Anthropic notes sentence/paragraph limits work better than word counts. | Moderate (vendor rule of thumb plus arithmetic); no published Gemini LOW or MINIMAL timing exists. Quality impact on financial answers is unmeasured. |
| 5 | **Merge, shrink or parallelise the router**: fold router and planning into one call, use a smaller or non-thinking model for routing, or start the likely query speculatively while routing runs | OpenAI's guide recommends parallelising, merging sequential LLM steps and speculative execution; Intercom community guidance puts ~7s per reasoning pass and advises fewer passes. Router savings of 1-3s with a lighter model is the researchers' estimate. | Weak to moderate: qualitative vendor advice and an anecdote; no benchmark isolates the gain, and only our own traces can size it. |
| 6 | **Stream the synthesis output** (or the answer text) once status events exist | Largest perception lever per the researchers' inference, since synthesis is the slow stage; thinking tokens still delay the first visible token. Structured JSON that is still arriving complicates validation. | Weak to moderate: vendor docs and practitioner posts; the "40-60% faster" figure is uncited and unused. |
| 7 | **Raise worker count or thread-pool capacity and keep clients singleton** if the investigation in item 2 confirms saturation | Shippo's load test improved success from 59% to 100% through threadpool, singleton client and sync handler changes (non-LLM service) ([Shippo](https://goshippo.com/blog/why-is-my-fastapi-throughput-so-low)). | Moderate for the mechanism; weak for our case until profiled. |

Two items the notes argue against prioritising: prompt caching, because halving input tokens yields only about 1-5% latency gain and Gemini's 4,096-token minimum means short router prompts will not cache; and semantic response caching, which gives large per-hit gains but carries staleness risk for changing financial values.

## Conclusion

The main insight is that the user concern is partly right and partly aimed at the wrong number. The median is a model-time floor that the best available evidence says users tolerate, and cutting it substantially means a faster model or fewer tokens, which carries quality risk for a financial-accuracy product. The part that is both worse than peers and cheap to fix is the experience of the wait: non-streamed delivery turns every 10-58s response into a blank screen, and both user-perception research and AWS operational guidance point to the same low-cost remedy, which also removes the ALB timeout failure class without touching model speed.

The largest open uncertainty is whether October's tail is real. A larger sample with per-phase timings and a verified model identity would show within days whether the regression stems from the 3.7-to-3.8 routing, provider variance or capacity saturation; until then, the October figures should be treated as a prompt to investigate rather than a finding. Also unresolved are user abandonment rates for financial-data waits (no source exists) and what the current front end displays during the wait.
